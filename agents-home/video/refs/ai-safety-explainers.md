Two visual eras:

- **Colorful (2023-24, S-Risks)**: saturated teal/orange/magenta palette; a round yellow dog mascot with glasses that
  reacts in a corner of the frame (thought bubbles); painterly skies and fire for stakes; cartoon portraits of real
  researchers with a name plate ("NICK BOSTROM / SWEDISH PHILOSOPHER") next to their paper's abstract on screen; a
  circuit-board motif as the background of "who says so" beats; a VHS "REW" overlay as a joke; a symbolic prop (a
  thread spool) carrying the abstract idea for several shots.
- **Noir (2025-26)**: near-monochrome greys plus one red accent that always means danger or power; heavy film grain
  on everything; soft glow on the accent; big single symbols (an eye with a raised-hand emblem, hands cupping the
  globe, a red dot that recurs as a signal); line-art characters with grey cel shading, elongated faces, large in
  frame for character beats (a reader with a banned book, stamp "ZAPRESHCHENO"); silhouettes and cities for scale;
  dates as plain glowing type ("2500 AD").

Per beat (both eras): one idea = one image held 3-4 s with slow internal motion; a recurring motif glues sections;
sources are shown as the real document (paper abstract, book cover) with a name plate, not as a citation line;
humour by props and overlays, not by the voice.

## What works
- One staged symbol per sentence; the narration never has to describe what the picture already says.
- Expert portraits + document on screen for authority beats.
- Palette discipline: one accent with one meaning; grain over flat vectors so nothing reads as clip-art.
- A mascot or recurring character the viewer can follow.

## What not to copy
- Their dog mascot, their long-faced noir cast, their eye/raised-hand emblem, the spool, the red-dot signal.
- Their exact palettes (teal circuit board; charcoal + red).
- 10-20 minute essay length.

## Not established
- Voice, music, sound design and pacing in motion (frames only, R14).
- Exact cut rate: 3-4 s sampling can miss cuts between samples.
